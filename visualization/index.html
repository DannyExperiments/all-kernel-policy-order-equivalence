Transition rows and policy order

Move the same transition probability p for both rewards. The signs of their advantages tell which action each reward prefers.

p = 2/5

RewardAdvantagePrefers
Reward 1−1/5Baseline
Reward 21/5Alternative

The rewards prefer opposite actions.

How to read the surfaces

Only the alternative action in one state receives reward. The baseline has value zero, so the alternative's Bellman advantage is its immediate expected reward. Both surfaces stay flat in the depth direction because the other transition rows do not affect these advantages.

Interior reversal uses successor rewards (1, −1) and (2, −1). At both deterministic endpoints the signs agree, but at p = 2/5 they differ. Boundary tie uses (1, 0) and (1, 1). They agree for p > 0 but have different tie behavior at zero.
