## Exact target and nearest results

- Skalse and Abate, *Partial Identifiability and Misspecification in Inverse Reinforcement Learning*, public arXiv v1, §4.1 Theorem 26 gives positive scaling, potential shaping and successor redistribution for a supplied kernel. Appendix A.7 Proposition 86 treats arbitrary distributions on trajectories, a stronger equivalence. The final paragraph explicitly leaves characterization of the all-kernel intersection to further analysis and gives the strict-separation example. The pinned Q717 fixes a finite discounted model, full-support start, and identical weak orders for all stationary randomized policies on every kernel. The published *Artificial Intelligence* article is volume 356 (2026), article 104525. The publisher metadata and indexed related text were checked, but direct final full-text download was inaccessible in this review. The theorem numbering and PDF page cited here refer to the accessible author version. ## Adjacencies and limits

- Ng, Harada and Russell (ICML 1999), and Jenner, van Hoof and Gleave (2022, Theorem 3), classify additive transformations that preserve optimal policies for every base reward and compatible dynamics, with their respective assumptions. The specific question here compares a fixed pair of reward tables using the full weak order. The scope of the state–action companion is the same weak-order target restricted to successor-independent rewards with at least two actions. Its positive affine necessity follows from the self-loop kernel and fixed redirect-to-anchor family, rather than a universal additive transformation theorem. [Ng et al.](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/icml99-shaping.pdf) · [Jenner et al. v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09570v2)
- Batra's 2026 *From Optimal Actions to World Models* fixes rewards as probes and characterizes kernels that are indistinguishable by their optimal actions over all such probes. Its quantifier orientation and observed object differ from Q717. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07301)
- General real quantifier elimination already gives decidability on algebraic inputs after writing the return comparison in polynomial terms. The result here gives explicit low-row-degree interpolation grids and a facewise sign-factor characterization that catches interior reversals and boundary ties. It supplies a finite criterion, with no complexity guarantee or general reward-transformation normal form. [Basu, Pollack and Roy](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-33099-2)

The bounded search covered the source, the dissertation, major canonicalization and shaping papers, later transition-identifiability results and generic real-algebraic decision methods. The state–action companion is part of the same catalog result.
